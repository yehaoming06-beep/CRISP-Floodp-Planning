## 5 Mathematical and evidence gate

The formulation distinguishes readiness, physical pump stock, abstract resources, HKD capital and CU expenses. Scenario-wise shared-stock accounting is coherent for mutually exclusive scenarios and one simultaneous deployment period. Zero recourse gives feasibility for budget-feasible first-stage policies in the unconstrained baseline. EEV fixes readiness and procurement before original-scenario recourse, supporting WS ≥ RP ≥ EEV. The EV objective is an artificial-scenario value and has no required ordering relative to RP or EEV.

Convention A's average derived bound and B's bound recomputed from averaged primitive data are different constructions. In the completed baselines they have identical primary-optimal policy sets and EEV/VSS ranges, while 6/26 legacy and 35/78 inventory fixed-policy EV objectives change under the 23-event frequencies. Their baseline agreement cannot rename the old convention-A suite as convention B.

For procurement alone, integer deployments imply that any feasible continuous n can be replaced by integer n* = max(0, max_s Σ_j x_sjD − N0) ≤ n, preserving y, x and the score while not increasing capital use. This establishes the reported projection equivalence under the current procurement relationships. It does not cover fractional deployments or other procurement mechanisms. The replicated/shared-stock witness establishes genuinely different feasible sets despite zero optimal-value differences in these instances.

The k = 6 control is infeasible because the Extreme scenario would require at least 12 deployment units, each using at least 42 labour units, so at least 504 > 400 labour units. Infeasibility is the expected boundary result, not a missing score to be filled with zero. No coverage or component information-value result should be invented.
